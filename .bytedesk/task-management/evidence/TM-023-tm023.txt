core.autocrlf=true confirmed in force for the clone.

    VERDICT: a fresh clone yields a readable board

Before the pin, every one of those 50 would have been CRLF and parsed to {}.

WHAT WENT WRONG ORIGINALLY. core.autocrlf converts on checkout, the fence stops
matching, and every document parses EMPTY - so tm board rendered
"undefined undefined - 0/19 done" while the .md files were perfectly intact. That
is what makes it dangerous: the obvious reading is "the store is corrupt", and it
is not. Measured at the time: 2 of 2 epics, 19 of 22 tasks and 10 of 25 ADRs
unreadable, while the single task the plugin had written AFTER the checkout was
LF and parsed fine.

I caused this instance with `git checkout -- .` while fixing the same class of
problem for shell scripts - a fair measure of how easy it is to hit.

RECOVERY, once the endings are right, is `tm reindex`: it rebuilds the index from
documents that were never damaged, and took the board straight back to
EP-002 16/22 done.

ALSO FIXED HERE: the previous .gitattributes edit went through a shell heredoc
that mangled its escapes, leaving carriage returns inside a comment. Git parsed
the broken lines as attribute names and warned on every invocation
("< is not a valid attribute name: .gitattributes:16"). Rewritten as a file.

THE DOCTOR CRITERION IS UPSTREAM. tm doctor reported this as
"orphan-epic: epic EP-002 does not exist" - a true observation and a misleading
diagnosis, since EP-002 existed and simply could not be parsed. Telling "absent"
from "unparseable" belongs in the plugin, which lives outside this repo. What is
in scope here is that check_line_endings.sh now catches the CRLF condition before
doctor gets the chance to misreport it.
